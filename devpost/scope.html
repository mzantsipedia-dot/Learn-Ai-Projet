Salary Check (working name)

A colourful little budgeting app that tells you whether your money will last until your next salary.

The heart of the idea

"Will my money last until the next salary?"

Most budgeting apps ask "did you go over your grocery limit?" This one compares money left with days left until the next salary. If it's two weeks after salary day and the cash is nearly gone, it turns red.

How it works: the core loop

Who it's for

You first: someone in South Africa whose salary gets "stretched thin" across debit orders, groceries, electricity and fuel.

Then family and friends: like the two friends whose cars broke down, who'd benefit from "knowing exactly where your money is and where you can cut costs."

What "working" looks like (the 1-minute demo)

One screen: pay date and salary at the top, fixed debit orders listed, expenses entered at the bottom. It shows what's spent, what's left, and the days until the next salary.

The "oh, that's useful" moment: add one more purchase, and the status turns red because the money won't last until the next salary.

What's in, what's later, what's cut

Click each one to open it.

Now: the proof of concept
  • Enter salary and pay date
  • Enter fixed monthly debit orders (around five)
  • Type in purchases: amount + category (groceries, electricity, fuel)
  • Show total spent, money left, days until the next salary
  • Red warning when money runs out faster than the days
  • Rand (R) currency, Klimt-inspired vibrant colours
Later: worth doing, just not now
  • Photograph receipts so the app reads them and categorises spending
  • Spot junk food and sweets, and suggest where you could have saved
  • 3-month comparison, with green when you spend less than last month
  • Sharing the app with family and friends
Cut: with reasons
  • Receipt scanning (for now): needs an AI image service, an account and secret key, and maybe a cost per scan. It would use up the 2–4 hours before the salary warning works.
  • South African store price comparisons: need live price data from stores, which is far beyond a proof of concept.

Look and feel

Inspired by Gustav Klimt, especially The Kiss: bright golds and yellows, reds, greens and starry blues. Numbers in South African rand, with clear highlighting.
